# What's Next?

## Outline

1. Intro
   - **Context**:
     The majority of our lives revolve around a screen. While the world is supposedly *more* connected, we, as a species, now feel more *disconnected* then ever in our existence.
   - **Intent**:
     I plan to evaluate **3** things in this [enter form of content here] a) to help discover how we got here, b) what is happening right now, and c) **What's Next**?
     1. *How we got here* - a 30,000 foot view into the breakthroughs of the last 50 years that have exponentially increased our 'connectivity', improved our quality of life, all while humans have become less happy, more lonely, and restless in the pursuit of purpose.
     2. *What is happening right now* - The information age has exposed humans to the answers to *all* of life's questions at the tap of a few fingers. While it would be ignorant to ignore the benefits of this advancement in tech, (emergency situations, convivence for extremely trivial daily tasks, etc.) it has certainly impaired the human ability to think critically, engage in deep, meaningful work, and altered our reliance (thus our ability) on using short-term memory.
     3. *What's Next?* - As humans grow more accustomed to living lives in tandem with technology, we will be exposed, without intent, (at least by our own individual effort) to the subconscious and conscious effects that ultra-dependency on technology has on us, for both deep, focused work, and, more excruciatingly, monotonous daily tasks that require little to no brain power -- but will require significant external stimulation.


   - **Key Message**   
   I have consumed a lot of media. You have consumed a lot of media. In a world that has become more connected, we have become less connected on the individual level. With ourselves, with family, with friends, we have less face-to-face interaction then ever before in human history. How did we get here? We can backtrack 20, 30, even 40+ years to see the evolution of the internet, personal mobile devices, and on-demand entertainment that fills up every second of our 'dull' (or even sometimes occupied) moments. But the more important questions isn't how we got here, it's: What's next?

     Over the course of this [enter media form here], we will discover proven short-term, and potential long-term (potential only because we have not lived long enough in the current state of technology to see how it effects people over multiple decades) outcomes of human beings in this ever-connected world. I feel compelled to write about this for a few reasons, which I will dive into later, but most of all because as I enter my adult years, I often find myself asking: What's next?
